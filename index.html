✨ What Charakuru Does for You

Most 3D avatar tools ask you to learn modeling software, rig a skeleton, and paint weights by hand. Charakuru skips that. You bring one illustration, and the kit walks you through the process of turning it into a finished avatar file that other apps can load.

The result is a VRM 1.0 avatar — a widely supported 3D character format used in games, streaming setups, and virtual worlds. Because the whole workflow is organized around a single drawing, you spend your time on the look of your character instead of the technical steps behind it.

Charakuru also lets you hand the work off to an AI agent. The kit is built so an agent can pick up the tasks, make the routine decisions, and return an avatar you can review — useful when you have many characters to produce and not much time.

🚀 Getting Started

You do not need any programming knowledge to use Charakuru. The app is organized into a short, guided sequence, and you can pause at any point to check your work.

Step 1 — Prepare your illustration

Use a clear drawing of one character, front-facing, with the whole body inside the frame. A plain background and even lighting help the kit read the shapes correctly. Save the file as a common image format such as PNG.

Step 2 — Import it into Charakuru

Open the app and choose the option to start a new character. Point it at your saved drawing. Charakuru reads the image and prepares a first draft of the avatar.

Step 3 — Review and adjust

Look over the draft. If a feature sits in the wrong place or a color looks off, use the adjustment controls to correct it. Small fixes here save a lot of time later.

Step 4 — Export your VRM avatar

When you are happy with the result, export the avatar. You will get a VRM 1.0 file you can load into any app that supports the format.

The written walkthrough with screenshots lives in the Tutorial. Work through it once and the rest of your characters will go much faster.

🎨 Key Features

  • One image in, one avatar out. Start from a single character illustration instead of a stack of reference sheets.
  • VRM 1.0 output. Export a standard avatar file that works with the apps and platforms you already use.
  • Share the work with an AI agent. Hand routine steps to an agent and review the finished character yourself.
  • Guided workflow. The app tells you what to do next, so there is no blank canvas to freeze in front of.
  • Repeatable results. Producing a second character is faster than the first because the steps stay the same.
  • Translated documentation. Guides are available in Japanese, Korean, and Simplified Chinese alongside English.

🖥️ System Requirements

Check that your computer meets these before you download. Meeting them keeps the avatar preview smooth and avoids long waits.

  • Operating system: Windows 10 or Windows 11, 64-bit
  • Memory: 8 GB or more (16 GB recommended for larger characters)
  • Storage: about 2 GB of free space for the app and your project files
  • Graphics: a modern graphics card with current drivers
  • Also needed: Node.js 22 or newer, and a stable internet connection for AI agent features

🧭 How the Workflow Fits Together

Charakuru is not a single button. It is a small set of stages, and each stage hands its result to the next one. Understanding the order helps when something does not look right.

First, the drawing is read and its shapes are separated from the background. Next, a three-dimensional form is built from those shapes. After that, the character is prepared so it can move and be posed. Finally the file is written out in VRM 1.0 format.

If you work with an AI agent, you can describe what you want at each stage and let the agent handle the routine parts. You still review the output, and you can always step in and change something by hand before the next stage begins.

❓ Frequently Asked Questions

Do I need to know 3D modeling?

No. The kit is designed so that someone with no modeling experience can go from a drawing to an avatar by following the guided steps.

What kind of drawing works best?

A single character, front-facing, full body, with a simple background. Clean linework and even colors give the most predictable result.

What is a VRM file?

It is a standard 3D avatar format. Think of it as a common file type, like JPG for images, but for characters that can be posed and animated.

Can I use the avatars I make?

Charakuru is source-available and comes with its own Terms of Use. Read them before you publish or sell anything you make.
